problem: "Studying children's language environments at scale requires safe, reliable and comparable audio from infants and toddlers, in places as different as a Paris apartment, a village in Vanuatu and a household in rural Namibia. Commercial recorders go out of production every few years, custom devices are very expensive, and an attachment that works for a baby in a stroller does not work for a baby carried on its mother's back."
constraints:
  - "Child safety and family acceptability are non-negotiable"
  - "Heat, humidity and dust in the field"
  - "Tight budgets, especially for teams in low- and middle-income countries"
  - "Data must still be comparable across sites"
approach: "I evaluated commercial and custom-built recorders, and the ways of attaching them, against technical performance, usability, child safety, family acceptability, environmental suitability and cost. I designed and coordinated iterative field pilots with partner teams in Argentina, Namibia, Bolivia and Vanuatu, after initial testing in Paris, and ran cross-site workshops so the teams could compare solutions in real conditions. The key insight was that no single product would work everywhere, so I defined a set of standard selection principles that local teams apply to their own context. I then scripted and narrated a training video, and commissioned its visuals, to teach those principles as practical guidance."
impact:
  metrics:
    - label: "Field pilots"
      value: "Argentina, Namibia, Bolivia, Vanuatu (+ Paris)"
    - label: "Largest deployment"
      value: "1,000-participant randomised controlled trial (Philippines)"
  qualitative: "Local teams can now balance recording quality, safety, usability, cultural fit and cost themselves. The principles produced context-specific solutions: hat-mounted recorders in Namibia to fit infant-carrying practices, and low-cost adapted clips for a 1,000-family randomised trial in the Philippines."
keyDecisions:
  - decision: "Standardise selection principles, not one product"
    reasoning: "Hardware lifecycles are short and contexts differ. A fixed device would be obsolete or wrong for some sites within a few years, while principles survive product changes and let local teams adapt."
    alternatives:
      - "Mandate one commercial device everywhere"
      - "Commission a custom device"
  - decision: "Iterate in the field with local teams instead of specifying solutions from the lab"
    reasoning: "Problems like carrying practices, heat and what families find acceptable only show up on site, and local teams know them best."
  - decision: "Teach through a short video rather than a written manual"
    reasoning: "Field staff work in many languages and with different levels of literacy. Showing how to attach a device works better than describing it."
learnings:
  - "[Edit] The right product decision was to stop looking for one product."
  - "[Edit] Cross-site workshops spread solutions faster than any guideline I could write."
